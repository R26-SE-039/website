# Planning Your Website — Designing a Website for Your Project

*SLIIT research project website guidelines (converted from the course PDF).*

---

## 1. Plan your website

- Create goals for your site
- Organize the site structure
- Create your design
- Design the navigation scheme
- Plan and gather your assets

**Plan everything BEFORE you start.**

---

## 2. Create goals for your site

- Deciding what your site goals are is the first step you take when creating a website.
- Ask yourself, or your group members, what you hope to accomplish.
- Write down your goals so that you remember them as you go through the design process.
- Goals help to focus and target your website needs.
- The website you develop should have a different look and navigation than a website that sells products.
- The complexity of your goals will affect the navigation.

---

## 3. Organize the site structure

- Organize your site carefully from the start.
- Begin by creating documents in a folder hierarchy.
- Create a folder on your local hard disk that contains all the files for your site (referred to as the
  *local site*), and create and edit documents within that folder.
- This is better than creating and editing files on the live public website itself, because it allows
  you to test changes in the local site before making them viewable to others.
- Break down your site into categories and put related pages in the same folder.
- Decide where to put items such as images and sound files (if any).

---

## 4. Create your design

- You will save a lot of time later if you plan your design and layout before you start building.
- You can simply create mock-up drawings on paper of how you want the site layout to look and then follow them.
- Maintain consistency in your page layout and design, which helps to ensure a good user experience.
- If all the pages have a different look, or the navigation is in a different place on each page,
  it might frustrate or confuse the user.
- Make sure your site provides a consistent look for your user.

---

## 5. Design the navigation scheme

- Think about the experience you want your visitors to have when navigating. Think about how a visitor
  will be able to move from one area to another. Navigation should be consistent throughout your site.
- Visitors should know where they are in the site and how to return to the top-level page.
- Search features and indexes make it easier for visitors to find information.

---

## 6. Plan and gather your assets

- Once you know your design and layout, you can create and gather the assets that you will need.
  Assets can be items such as text, images or media.
- Make sure you have gathered all of these items and they are ready to go before you start developing your site.
- If you are using images and graphics from clip-art, or someone else is creating them, make sure you
  collect them and put them in a folder on your site. If you are creating the assets yourself, make sure
  you have created them all, including any images you need, before you start development. Then organize
  your assets so you can access them easily while creating your site.

---

## 7. Common structure

The following structure should be used for the main tab area. Refer to the websites that your seniors
have created: http://cdap.sliit.lk/

- Home
- Domain
- Milestones
- Documents
- Slides of past presentations
- About us
- Contact us

---

## 8. Section requirements

### 8.1 Home

This gives a general introduction to the project, in abstract, in a concise and clear form.
It should be very attractive; therefore, the designer is free to use suitable graphics related to the project.

**Layout and design:**
- A good home page doesn't overuse images and graphics, and has a consistent layout and organization.
- The more general the page, the more important it is to have navigational aids and tables of contents
  (if possible — optional).
- Do not use slow-loading graphics.
- Optimize the graphics you use by making them smaller and using few colors.

### 8.2 Domain

This section has domain details about your project. Describe the information you have gathered
through the literature survey.

- Literature survey
- Research gap
- Research problem
- Research objectives
- Methodology
- Technologies used

### 8.3 Milestones

Describe all assessments in the project. When describing items in the list, include details of the
assessment, the date and the marks allocated for each item. When designing, use a **drop-down menu**
for the user to choose.

- Project Proposal
- Progress Presentation 1
- Progress Presentation 2
- Final Assessment
- Viva, etc.

### 8.4 Documents

This section should list all documents you have already produced and those pending
(**provide only links**) for users to view.

- Project Charter (one document)
- Proposal Document (one document)
- Checklist documents
- Final Documents (4 documents with the main document), etc.

### 8.5 Presentations (slides)

This section provides the slides used in past presentations. Keep provision for future presentations.

- Proposal presentation
- Progress Presentation 1
- Progress Presentation 2
- Final presentation, etc.

### 8.6 About us

- Group members
- Identification photograph (if any)
- Contact details (e-mail)
- Any relevant information (achievements, etc.)

### 8.7 Contact us

- General contact phone numbers and e-mails
- General e-mail template

## 10. Compliance checklist (derived from the guidelines above)

- [ ] Site goals written down before building
- [ ] Local folder hierarchy; related pages and assets grouped
- [ ] Mock-up of the layout prepared before development
- [ ] Same layout, navbar position and look on every page
- [ ] Visitors can always see where they are and return to Home
- [ ] All assets (text, images, logos, photos) gathered and organized before development
- [ ] Tabs: Home, Domain, Milestones, Documents, Presentations, About us, Contact us
- [ ] Home: attractive, concise abstract, few optimized graphics, no slow-loading images
- [ ] Domain: literature survey, research gap, research problem, objectives, methodology, technologies
- [ ] Milestones: details, date and marks for each assessment, chosen from a drop-down
- [ ] Documents: links only; produced and pending documents listed
- [ ] Presentations: past slides plus provision for future presentations
- [ ] About us: members, photos, e-mails, achievements
- [ ] Contact us: phone numbers, e-mails, e-mail template
- [ ] Total upload <= 20 MB; output is static HTML/CSS (confirm with the coordinator whether the small
      JavaScript files produced by a static export are acceptable)
